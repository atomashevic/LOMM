\begin{center}
{\Large Response to the Editor and Reviewer}\\[0.5em]
Manuscript: \textit{A Latent Oscillator Measurement Model to Simulate Emotional-Expression Score Dynamics in Video}
\end{center}

Dear Dr. Epskamp, Editors, and Reviewer,

Thank you for the careful reading and for the guidance on scope, structure, and examples. We revised the paper as a methods contribution centered on the Latent Oscillator Measurement Model (LOMM). The revision presents LOMM as a generator for model-derived emotional-expression score series, adds a selected realism check against MAFW-derived classifier-output panels, and evaluates recovery of a known structure on pre-link linear block-measurement panels. The public observation pipeline now defaults to a bounded-score configuration with empirical between-clip baselines and clip-specific scale (B+S); within-loading-space B+S and the exactly nested homogeneous profile remain explicit alternatives.

Two scope decisions guided the revision. First, we renamed the model \textit{Latent Oscillator Measurement Model} because damping is one velocity-feedback regime in LOMM; the model also permits zero or positive velocity feedback. The model section states that negative velocity feedback produces damped return, zero velocity feedback removes damping, and positive velocity feedback can sustain or amplify change over the finite interval. This framing better fits short video time series, where brief expressive episodes in the empirical MAFW example may show bursts, switching, or low expressivity without clearly exhibiting damping. Second, we moved tutorial material out of the paper because the \textit{transforEmotion} R package tutorial has already been published separately and is cited in the revised Introduction. The present manuscript focuses on the model, selected realism targets, and benchmark.

The manuscript defines MAFW at first substantive mention as a large-scale, multimodal, compound affective database for dynamic facial-expression recognition in the wild.

The appendices carry the technical support. Appendix B gives the perturbation-target argument for the recovery methods on the historical homogeneous revision-v2 linear benchmark, and Appendix C gives numeric empirical-realism checks. Appendix D reports trajectory-recovery diagnostics for method-network result rows with recovered score summaries, Appendix E gives runtime and feasibility accounting, and Appendix F reports scenario parameter values.

Because line numbers can change during production, we refer below to sections, figures, tables, and pages in the rendered revised manuscript.

  \item \point{Realism of LOMM simulations}
  \begin{reviewquote}

  \response We added a historical revision-v2 empirical-realism construction check comparing simulated datasets with MAFW-derived classifier score series. That check evaluates six targets---channel IQR, derivative IQR, lag-1 autocorrelation, run-length entropy, spectral entropy, and effective rank---at observed-channel and recovered community-score-summary network results. The later paired B/S/G adoption analysis is a separate five-fold analysis of eight probability-scale and the corresponding eight log-ratio metrics on the retained 100-clip score scale. Its fold-stratified clip-cluster percentile intervals are descriptive; the B and S point estimates fall below their respective intervals because the nonlinear discrepancy estimands show bootstrap bias. B gave the clearest descriptive discrepancy reduction, scale gave a smaller uncertain marginal reduction, and those results motivated the pipeline decision to use configurable B+S as the public compositional default. The completed dimensional-recovery grid uses its historical homogeneous pre-link linear generator, and the within-loading-space bridge uses \texttt{linear\_z}. Neither recovery analysis validates recovery from bounded probabilities or CLR panels.

  The main Results summarize worst-case accuracy, assignment accuracy, successful-fit runtime, and valid, timeout, and error status counts. The Results and Appendix D report trajectory-recovery \(r\) and NRMSE among method-network result rows with recovered score summaries, excluding failed or timed-out fits.

  Appendix B formalizes the historical homogeneous revision-v2 continuous-indicator recovery target as a graph-perturbation problem. DynEGA and GraphicalVAR read the block signal through partial-correlation graphs of derivative or VAR innovation network results. GIMME is treated as an indirect graph-conversion pipeline with completion included in worst-case recovery. Figure B1 plots both network result maps against the filtered signal-to-noise ratio with the matched measurement grid marked. The shared perturbation margin also predicts the scenario-family ordering through the activity gains for the population network result pipelines, and Appendix B reports conditional hit rates among usable network results alongside the worst-case rates. The derivation does not cover empirical-baseline compositions, CLR panels, or the within-loading-space bridge's between-clip mean structure.
